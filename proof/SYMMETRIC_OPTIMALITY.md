# Optimality and uniqueness within the D3h-symmetric class

## Scope and theorem

**This proves a restricted mathematical theorem, not the unrestricted N=9
Thomson theorem.** The orbit classification, phase comparison, and planar
exclusion below are not yet formalized in Lean. The exact candidate parameter,
coordinate energy formula, and unique minimum of the one-parameter family are
Lean-checked; see `lean/verification/`.

Let `G` be the subgroup of `O(3)` generated by rotation through `2*pi/3` about
the z-axis and the two reflections

\[
 V(x,y,z)=(x,-y,z),\qquad H(x,y,z)=(x,y,-z).
\]

This is the standard geometric `D3h` action, of order 12. Saying a configuration
has this symmetry means that its **unlabeled set** is invariant under an
orthogonal conjugate of `G`; it does not require its full symmetry group to
equal `G`.

**Theorem.** Among nine distinct points on `S^2` with this symmetry, the exact
candidate `P9` has minimum Coulomb energy. Equality occurs exactly for its
orthogonal images and relabelings.

## Exact family already established

Put

\[
 u_0=(1,0),\quad u_1=(-1/2,\sqrt3/2),\quad
 u_2=(-1/2,-\sqrt3/2),\quad h=\sqrt{1-r^2}.
\]

For `0<r<1`, define the anti-aligned family by the six points `(r u_k,+/-h)`
and the three equatorial caps `(-u_k,0)`. Its energy, with each pair counted
once, is

\[
 F(r)=\sqrt3+\frac{2\sqrt3}{r}+\frac3{2\sqrt{1-r^2}}
      +\frac6{\sqrt{4-r^2}}+\frac6{\sqrt{2+2r}}
      +\frac{12}{\sqrt{2-r}}.
\]

The rotation cycles the three indices, `V` exchanges indices 1 and 2, and `H`
exchanges the two prism levels. Hence this family is `G`-invariant. Unit norms
and distinctness for `0<r<1` are proved in `Candidate.lean`.

The exact radius `r_*` is the unique zero of `F'` in the rational interval
specified in `N9_CANDIDATE.md` and `lean/ThomsonN9/Candidate.lean`. Its existence
and uniqueness are proved by exact endpoint signs, continuity, and strict
monotonicity of `F'`. No numerical energy or optimizer output is an assumption.
The Lean theorem `candidate_family_minimizer` establishes

\[
 F(r)\ge F(r_*),\qquad
 F(r)=F(r_*)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ r=r_*\quad(0<r<1),
\]

and identifies these values with the coordinate-defined Coulomb energies.

## Exhaustive orbit classification

After an orthogonal change of coordinates, assume the set is `G`-invariant.
For a point off the z-axis write its azimuth as `theta`. Rotation and `V` act
on azimuth by `theta -> theta+2*pi/3` and `theta -> -theta`. This azimuth orbit
has size 3 exactly when `theta` is a multiple of `pi/3`; otherwise it has size
6. The horizontal reflection independently changes the sign of nonzero `z`.
Consequently every point falls into exactly one row:

| Location | Additional condition | Orbit size |
| --- | --- | --- |
| z-axis | the two unit poles | 2 |
| equator | mirror azimuth, `theta` a multiple of `pi/3` | 3 |
| equator | non-mirror azimuth | 6 |
| off equator and off axis | mirror azimuth | 6 |
| off equator and off axis | non-mirror azimuth | 12 |

There is exactly one axis orbit, containing both poles. There are exactly two
size-3 orbits: the equatorial triangles `A={u_0,u_1,u_2}` and `B=-A`. There
are no other orbit sizes, including no fixed points on the sphere.

A nine-point invariant set is a disjoint union of these orbits. A size-12 orbit
cannot occur. The axis orbit cannot occur either: every remaining orbit has
size divisible by 3, but `9-2=7` is not divisible by 3. Three size-3 orbits are
impossible because only two such orbits exist. Hence the decomposition is
necessarily **6+3**.

If the size-6 orbit is equatorial, every point lies on the same great circle.
Otherwise it has the form `(r u_k,+/-h)` after a rotation by a multiple of
`pi/3`, with `0<r<1`. The endpoints would collapse the orbit and are excluded
by distinctness. The remaining size-3 orbit consists either of the aligned
caps `(u_k,0)` or the anti-aligned caps `(-u_k,0)`. This exhausts the symmetry
class; no symmetry assumption about a general minimizer has been inferred.

## The aligned phase is strictly worse

Let

\[
 g(t)=(2-2t)^{-1/2},\qquad b(t)=g(t)-g(-t).
\]

Only the 18 prism-cap interactions change between the two phases. The aligned
phase has inner products `r` six times and `-r/2` twelve times. The anti-aligned
phase has `-r` six times and `r/2` twelve times. Therefore

\[
 E_{\rm aligned}(r)-F(r)=6\bigl(b(r)-2b(r/2)\bigr).
\]

Here `b(0)=0` and, for `0<t<1`,

\[
 b''(t)=3\left((2-2t)^{-5/2}-(2+2t)^{-5/2}\right)>0.
\]

Thus `b` is strictly convex on `[0,r]`. Its strict midpoint inequality gives
`b(r/2)<b(r)/2`, so `E_aligned(r)>F(r)>=F(r_*)`. This is an exact inequality
for every allowed radius, not a check at the candidate radius alone.

## A rational upper bound for the candidate

The proved family minimum implies `E(P9)<=F(71/100)`. The following strict
square-root bounds follow by squaring positive rational endpoints:

\[
 \sqrt3<1733/1000,\quad
 \sqrt{4959/10000}>704/1000,\quad
 \sqrt{34959/10000}>1869/1000,
\]
\[
 \sqrt{342/100}>1849/1000,\qquad
 \sqrt{129/100}>1135/1000.
\]

The five positive differences between the squared quantities in the required
directions are, respectively,
`3289/1000000`, `71/250000`, `2739/1000000`, `1199/1000000`, and `71/40000`.
Substitution gives

\[
 F(71/100)
 <\frac{1733}{1000}\left(1+\frac{200}{71}\right)
   +\frac{375}{176}+\frac{2000}{623}
   +\frac{6000}{1849}+\frac{2400}{227}
 =\frac{10526945344833259}{408443364098000}
 <\frac{129}{5}.
\]

The last strict inequality has positive difference
`10893448895141/408443364098000`. This deliberately loose bound suffices for
the planar exclusion and needs no high-precision numerical energy estimate.

## Every planar alternative is excluded

Order nine distinct points on their unit great circle cyclically. Let the nine
positive angular gaps be `alpha_i`, summing to `2*pi`, and their adjacent chord
lengths be `d_i=2*sin(alpha_i/2)`. Then

\[
 \sum_i d_i\le\sum_i\alpha_i=2\pi<13/2.
\]

For completeness, the bound on `pi` can be certified by the elementary identity

\[
 0<\int_0^1\frac{x^4(1-x)^4}{1+x^2}\,dx=\frac{22}{7}-\pi,
 \qquad 22/7<13/4.
\]

Polynomial division gives the integrand as
`x^6-4x^5+5x^4-4x^2+4-4/(1+x^2)`, verifying the integral identity.
The reciprocal form of Cauchy-Schwarz now gives

\[
 \sum_{i=1}^9\frac1{d_i}\ge\frac{81}{\sum_i d_i}>\frac{162}{13}.
\]

These cyclic edges are nine different unordered pairs. Each of the other
`36-9=27` pairs has distance at most 2 and contributes at least `1/2`. Thus

\[
 E(X)>\frac{162}{13}+\frac{27}{2}=\frac{675}{26}
      >\frac{129}{5}>E(P9),
\]

where `675/26-129/5=21/130>0`.

## Equality and Scope

The planar case is strictly excluded, as is the aligned cap phase. In the sole
remaining anti-aligned family, equality requires `r=r_*` by the proved strict
family minimum. The orbit normalization then identifies the unlabeled set with
an orthogonal image of `P9`; restoring labels gives a permutation. Conversely,
orthogonal transformations preserve every pair distance, and permutations
reindex the 36 pairs, so they preserve energy. This proves both directions of
the equality statement in the restricted theorem.

The restricted theorem above does not by itself force an arbitrary low-energy
configuration into this symmetry class. That global step is now supplied by
the separate tiny-residual SOS and Gram-localization argument in
`GLOBAL_PROOF.md`. This restricted result is not a substitute for the separately
compiled unrestricted Lean theorem described in `FORMAL_PROOF_GUIDE.md`.
The orbit-classification argument in this document is a mathematical alternative,
not a claimed dependency of that formal theorem.
